# Copy & Conversion — how the words earn the signup

Every page is governed by one equation: **Purchase Rate = Desire − (Labor + Confusion).** Every copy and layout decision either raises desire, cuts labor (effort the visitor must spend), or cuts confusion. If a change does none of those, it doesn't ship. Design decorates; copy converts.

## Step 1 — Copy strategy spec (before writing a single headline)

Produce this spec in the plan, in plain language Joe can react to:

1. **The buyer, in one line.** For Afterkey: a builder doing 5–50 homes a year whose phone is the warranty department.
2. **Top 3 buying objections**, ranked. Mine them from the brand file, Joe's own sales conversations, and voice-of-customer sources (below). Afterkey's usual three: "my subs won't use software," "my homeowners will just call me anyway," "another subscription — what does it actually replace?"
3. **The single promise** the page makes — one sentence, no commas if possible.
4. **The CTA narrative**: what the button continues, not what it says. The CTA is the next sentence of the headline's story.
5. **Proof inventory**: what real proof exists (screenshots, Joe's own builder credibility, founder story, the pricing). What doesn't exist yet is intentionally omitted, never faked.

## Headline rules

- **Litmus test:** if a stranger reads only the headline and the button, do they know what this is, who it's for, and what to do? If any of the three is missing, rewrite.
- Formula that works for B2B tools: **[outcome the buyer wants] + [the mechanism, in their words]**. Not "Streamline post-closing operations" but "Warranty callbacks handled without your cell phone."
- Sub-headline carries the mechanism and the who: one or two sentences, concrete nouns.
- Never lead with the product name or "Introducing." Lead with the buyer's situation.

## Page anatomy that converts (in order)

1. **Hero** — headline, sub, one primary CTA, one lower-commitment secondary (the quiz or "see how it works"), one real proof element if it exists. Half the effort goes here. No carousel, no three CTAs.
2. **The problem, in the buyer's words** — two or three short beats. Voice-of-customer language, not marketing language. This is where the reader decides you understand them.
3. **How it works** — 3–4 steps, each a verb the buyer or their homeowner takes. Real product screenshots. Numbering here is fine because it IS a sequence.
4. **Objection handling** — one section per top objection, headed by the objection itself as a plain statement ("Your subs don't want another app."). Answer in two sentences with the mechanism.
5. **Proof** — only what's real. Founder credibility ("built by a builder") is proof. Pricing transparency is proof. Empty testimonial slots are not.
6. **Pricing** — stated openly, with the per-home fee framed as baked into the home's cost at closing (Afterkey rule). Anchoring line: what one callback costs vs. the monthly price.
7. **Final CTA** — restate the promise, repeat the primary CTA, add the low-friction alternative (quiz).

Each section has one job; if a section is doing two, split it or cut one.

## CTA rules

- Primary CTA continues the story: "Start handling callbacks" / "See a callback handled in 10 seconds" — never "Get started," "Learn more," "Submit."
- One primary CTA per page, repeated; secondary CTAs are visibly lower-weight.
- Button text under 5 words; the line under the button removes a fear ("No card. 5-minute setup.") only if true.
- Form fields: ask for the minimum that lets Joe follow up. Every extra field is Labor.

## Voice-of-customer mining (do this, don't guess)

Before writing objection or problem copy, pull real phrasing from where the buyer talks: builder forums and subreddits, NAHB and builder-association content, reviews of adjacent tools (Buildertrend, CoConstruct, warranty-management products), and Joe's own words about running Pleasantview. Capture 10–15 verbatim phrases about the post-closing problem; use their nouns and verbs. The page should sound like the buyer describing his own week, not like a vendor describing features.

## Frameworks to reach for

- **PAS** (problem → agitate → solve) for the problem section and the quiz result pages.
- **Objection-first** for the middle of the page.
- **Before/after** for the hero sub or a single contrast section (the phone-at-9pm builder vs. the builder who checks a dashboard).
