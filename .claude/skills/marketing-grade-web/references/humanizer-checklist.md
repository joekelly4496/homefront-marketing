# Humanizer Checklist — making the words sound like a person

Run this pass on every piece of visible text before it ships: headlines, body, buttons, form labels, error messages, quiz questions, result pages, and emails. The patterns below are drawn from the community-maintained catalog of signs of AI writing (originally compiled by Wikipedia editors who spend their days spotting generated text). Readers can't name these patterns but they feel them, and the feeling is "nobody's home."

## Pass 1 — Vocabulary (delete on sight)

delve · landscape (as in "the marketing landscape") · leverage (verb) · seamless · robust · streamline · empower · unlock · elevate · game-changer · revolutionize · cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · holistic · synergy · navigate (metaphorical) · tapestry · testament to · underscore · foster · crucial · pivotal · vibrant · nuanced · multifaceted · in today's fast-paced world · at the end of the day · it's worth noting · solutions (as a noun for "what we sell") · AI-powered (as a benefit)

Replace with the plain word a builder would use, or cut the sentence.

## Pass 2 — Structural tics

- **Negative parallelism**: "It's not about X, it's about Y." / "This isn't just a tool, it's a…" → state Y directly.
- **Rule of three**: three adjectives, three benefits, three anything, when the real number is two or four. Use the true count.
- **Em-dash overuse**: more than one per paragraph is a tell. Use a period or a comma.
- **Connector words opening sentences**: Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Ultimately, In conclusion → cut; the sentences connect on their own.
- **Inflated significance**: "plays a vital role," "a key part of," "critical to the success of" → say what it does.
- **Vague attribution**: "experts agree," "studies show," "industry leaders" → name the source or cut the claim.
- **Hedging stacks**: "may potentially help to" → "helps," or say it doesn't.
- **Colon headlines**: "Warranty Management: A New Approach" → no colons in headlines.
- **Title Case Everything** → sentence case for headings and buttons.
- **Summary sentences** that restate the paragraph you just read → delete.
- **Uniform sentence length** → vary it. A short one lands.
- **Perfect symmetry**: every section the same shape, every card the same three bullets → break it where the content is uneven.

## Pass 3 — Content honesty

- No claim the business can't back today. No invented numbers, quotes, or customers (see the honesty rules in SKILL.md).
- Specifics over abstractions: "the 11-month walkthrough" beats "key milestones." If a sentence has no concrete noun, it's probably filler.
- Say the price. Say what it doesn't do. Real companies have edges.

## Pass 4 — Voice matching (do this for every brand)

Before the first draft, pull 2–3 paragraphs of the founder's own writing (Joe's emails, his posts, how he explains Afterkey out loud) and note: sentence length, favorite verbs, how he opens, whether he uses "you" or "builders," what he never says. Write to that profile. For Afterkey, the afterkey-content skill already encodes the voice and the banned list — apply both.

## Pass 5 — Read it aloud test

Read each section as if saying it to a builder at a job site. Anything you'd be embarrassed to say out loud gets rewritten. That single test catches most of what the lists above catch.

## On AI-detection tools

Don't optimize against detectors (GPTZero, Originality, Copyleaks). They disagree with each other, false-flag human writing, and pass polished AI text. The goal is not to fool a classifier; it's for a builder to feel a person wrote this. The checklist above is the whole method.
